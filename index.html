01 · the problem

Every organisation above a certain size is partially amnesiac

It carries the consequences of past decisions without any memory of why they were made. Three failures compound into this. The same supplier lives under a dozen different keys in a dozen systems, and nothing reconciles them. Systems record states, never transitions, and never the reasoning behind a change. And most of what an organisation knows is held in people, never written down at all.

$250M a year is the labour a typical Fortune 500 company wastes on ineffective decision-making, roughly 530,000 manager-days. Source: McKinsey, 2019.
  • $12.9M a year is the average cost of poor data quality to an organisation. Gartner, 2021
  • Nearly a fifth of the work week is spent searching for information the company already holds somewhere. McKinsey, 2012

No system on the market treats time as a structural dimension. Everything on offer retrieves fragments of the present. None of it answers the question a CEO actually has: what changed, when, why, and what will the next decision propagate into?

02 · what we build

Three capabilities, in strict order

  1. first · ships now

    Evidence reconstruction

    Forensics on roughly two years of a company's own record: its communications, the ERP and ledger, the commerce exports. Entities, events, and decisions land in a bi-temporal knowledge graph that remembers both when something was true and when the company learned it. Every answer is auditable down to its exact source. Where the rationale was never written down, structured elicitation interviews with the executives who carry it become consented, attributed, queryable sources.

  2. second · the real product

    Live capture

    Recording decisions, exceptions, approvals, and their stated rationale at the moment they happen, before the context evaporates. This is what finally reaches the knowledge that today lives only in people.

  3. third · the headline, earned

    Simulation

    Forecasting the cross-functional ripple of a decision before it is made. Never pitched on faith. The system first runs a shadow period in which its forecasts are checked silently against what actually happens. The client sees the track record before being asked to trust a forecast.

The order is the discipline. Simulation over a poorly resolved record produces confident nonsense, so it sits last, behind the quality layer and the validation period.

03 · the four questions

Built around four query classes, not generic search

query class · 1

Point-in-time reconstruction

"What did our supplier base for the snacks range look like just before Diwali 2024?"

query class · 2

Change tracing

"When did margin on our hero SKU start slipping, and what changed near that point?"

query class · 3

Decision forensics

"Who approved the marketplace discount, what was the stated reason, and what did it touch?"

query class · 4

Precedent search

"Have we made this kind of pricing exception before, and what happened after?"

These are the questions a CEO asks today and answers by sending five people away for a week.
